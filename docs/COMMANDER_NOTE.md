# Note to commanders

Your role is to shape the battlefield and create situations for the DCS
players. There is currently no score or scripted victory condition. DCS decides
which units survive and which coalition owns each airbase.

## A useful command cycle

1. **Observe.** Check your bases, forces, aircraft, and recent orders. An
   accepted order means DCS received it; it does not guarantee success.
2. **Choose one objective.** Concentrate forces rather than filling the map with
   unrelated groups.
3. **Attack an airbase.** An assault creates a combined-arms ground force and
   sends it toward the selected base. Players can support or stop that force.
4. **Secure a captured base.** Once DCS reports the base as yours, use **Defend
   base** for a mixed defensive force. Add smaller defenses within the permitted
   area when they serve a clear purpose.
5. **Use air power deliberately.** Keep routine CAP available, scramble parked
   ready fighters when needed, and recover surviving aircraft at friendly
   bases. Aircraft, fuel, weapons, losses, and combat remain part of DCS.
6. **Reassess.** Watch what actually happens before issuing the next order.
   Enemy players and AI should be able to change your plan.

## Current controls

- **Attack base** sends a randomized armored or mechanized force toward a
  neutral or enemy strategic airbase.
- **Defend base** places a mixed force of armor, infantry, short-range air
  defense, and a SAM system at a strategic base that DCS reports as yours.
- **Additional deployment** places one smaller curated defense package near an
  owned strategic base.
- **Scramble CAP** starts a cold, parked alert flight at Batumi or Gudauta. Its
  route and combat behavior are already defined in the DCS mission.
- Unit orders can move or hold ground forces, change fire permission, redirect
  aircraft, assign an air mission, or send aircraft to a friendly recovery
  base.

## Command intent

Give players problems, opportunities, and visible consequences. Leave enough
time for them to react. Protecting an assault, intercepting a CAP, destroying a
SAM, or preventing an airbase capture should materially change what happens
next.

Air reinforcement of newly captured bases is planned but is not a command yet.
For now, captured bases can receive ground defenses, while the two ready fighter
flights remain at their original home bases.
